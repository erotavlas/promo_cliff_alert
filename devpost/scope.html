Subscription Tracker with Negotiation Coach

A mobile-friendly app that tracks your subscriptions, warns you when a promo ends or a price rises, then helps you negotiate a better price using what other people actually got.

The unique kernel

Most trackers stop at "your price is going up." This one goes on to what to do about it: crowdsourced negotiation results feed an LLM, which writes a call script grounded in real outcomes.

How it fits together

Who it's for

Anyone with recurring subscriptions (internet, TV, phone, gym, streaming) who loses track of dates and notices only when a bill jumps. The origin story: a 2-year Bell Canada internet promo expired unnoticed, the bill rose about $10–20/month, and a long phone call was needed to get a new promo.

What "working" looks like

In the demo video: open the app, see the "needs attention" area, tap a Bell internet subscription whose promo is ending, see current price in green vs. new price in red (monthly and yearly), tap Negotiate to see what others got, then generate a script to read on the phone. The "oh, that's cool" beat is the script built on real outcomes.

The proof-of-concept boundary

Now (in the POC)
  • Manual add-subscription form: category, monthly cost, discount/promo, promo end date, post-promo price
  • Subscription list with a "needs attention" area sorted by date
  • Detail screen: current vs. new price, monthly and yearly, green/red indicators
  • Negotiate screen with pre-written (seeded) sample results
  • LLM-generated negotiation script informed by those results
  • Mobile-sized web app
Later
  • Background push notifications and the "emergency only / all updates" setting
  • Timeline or calendar view
  • Email-bill monitoring
  • Cheaper-plan suggestions (e.g. ad-supported tiers)
  • Live crowdsourcing: users submit results to a shared database (you plan to add this yourself after the hackathon)
Explicitly cut
  • Native phone app: a mobile-sized web app shows the same thing faster
  • Real user accounts: not needed to prove the kernel
  • Moderating crowdsourced entries: only matters once live submissions exist
  • Live crowdsourcing for the hackathon: seeded examples fit the time budget
